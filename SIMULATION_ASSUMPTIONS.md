# Simulation assumptions

MF-001 is a deterministic training scenario. Identical configuration, elapsed time and incident choices reproduce the same run. Operators inject incidents. There is no randomized failure probability.

- A sol lasts 24.6597 hours. Manual steps integrate at intervals of at most one hour. The clock stops at the planned duration or when a critical resource reaches zero.
- Solar generation uses installed kW × mean daily POWER solar irradiation / 24. This daily average omits night cycles, tracking, seasonal variation and AC losses. Mars and Moon projections use illustrative 0.43 and 0.60 solar scaling factors. These are training projections, not local lunar or Martian illumination forecasts. Gravity values are displayed and used for illustrative crew gait only; atmospheric pressure, rigid-body dynamics and physiological radiation effects are outside the simulation.
- Habitat demand rises when exterior temperature moves more than 15 °C from the selected scenario baseline. This is an illustrative heating/cooling load, not a thermal engineering model. The rover has a 2.4 kWh pack, draws 0.12 kW while moving, and recharges from habitat power at up to 0.32 kW while stationary. A communications outage or wind above 25 m/s suspends driving. Generated energy serves loads and then charges storage up to its capacity. Battery loss represents net discharge. Backup power supplies up to 20 kWh when enabled.
- The live dust control reduces modeled solar input and adds scene haze. Wind at or above 25 m/s together with dust opacity at or above 45% starts an automatic storm drill that suspends surface work; this threshold is a training rule. Dust above 65% adds illustrative habitat wear. A lunar dust setting represents a local plume; the Moon has no atmospheric wind. The displayed radiation dose is the entered daily rate integrated over elapsed time; passing 20 mSv issues a review warning, not a health prediction. Facility switches hide the corresponding models and pause their station work. Pressurized passages are illustrative and do not model airlocks, pressure loss or structural engineering.
- Each crew member consumes 0.84 kg oxygen, 1.6 L net water and 0.64 kg food per 24 hours. Values represent training budgets. An oxygen leak adds 0.38 kg/h, or 0.09 kg/h after isolation. One emergency reserve adds 3 kg.
- A solar failure leaves 15% generation, a storm leaves 35%, and dust opacity reduces generation by up to 70%. Effects multiply. Repairs consume spares. Shedding nonessential load reduces base demand to 65%. Sheltering through a dust storm advances time by four hours.
- A dispatched rover travels at an illustrative average 0.22 km/h. Local scene motion illustrates activity around the base. Breakdowns and dust suspend travel.
- ASTRA uses fictional crew, synthetic vital signs and scripted medical drills. Time on shift, activity and incident choice change readings. The medical kit and reduced duty create follow-up monitoring intervals. These exercises have no clinical or treatment authority.
- The report snapshots actual reserves and the recorded decision log. Habitat endurance requires reaching the scheduled end. A rover or geological objective also requires positive traverse distance.

Crew duty cycles include 0.20 hours of outbound travel, 0.55 hours of work and 0.20 hours for the return. Each completed work period records one sample, audit or suit check, or recovers 0.35 L of water up to tank capacity. Storms halt field work. Medical incidents and recovery suspend the affected astronaut’s duties. The helmet and chase cameras track the same duty progress used to calculate production. The ASTRA pulse trace is a synthetic rhythm paced by the selected astronaut’s simulated heart rate.

The pre-launch transfer animation is illustrative. Countdown, ascent and separation do not model launch dynamics or transport to the selected field site.
The 3D sequence combines a historical launch vehicle with a fictional field lander. Habitat integrity tracks illustrative exposure, falling 0.35 percentage points per simulated hour during a leak and 0.18 during dust.

## Facility exposure and maintenance

Earth, Mars, and Moon modes use separate scenario inputs on the selected Earth analog terrain. Inputs are hypothetical and the pressure readout is a representative scenario constant. Earth humidity drives a provisional corrosion index. Airborne dust and cloud cover reduce relative illumination. On the Moon, local dust can settle on equipment under vacuum without wind or atmospheric extinction.

The engine computes atmospheric transmission using an exponential optical-depth term and separately models deposited dust as a saturating coverage fraction. The separation follows [Mars Exploration Rover power operations](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230007029). The scenario-to-optical-depth mapping, deposition and wear rates, thermal-cycle fatigue indices, maintenance efficacy, and failure thresholds are demonstration coefficients. They have not been fitted to mission telemetry or laboratory materials data. The reported damage indices and offline thresholds are deterministic scenario estimates.

The mission integrator updates exposure in steps of at most one hour. Surface contamination persists when suspended dust clears. Cleaning removes most of the deposited dust. Replacement lowers accumulated damage on the serviced assembly, advances the mission clock and consumes generic spares on completion. Historical replay reconstructs the most recent 24 logged event checkpoints recorded by the exposure engine. Earth analog terrain, Earth Viewer observations and user-controlled scenarios retain their separate roles.
